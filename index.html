The problem

The interception is happening now. Only the decryption is deferred.

Shor's algorithm destroys RSA, Diffie-Hellman and elliptic-curve cryptography outright. Grover's algorithm merely weakens symmetric ciphers, which is answered by doubling key lengths. The consequence is asymmetric: symmetric cryptography needs a parameter change, asymmetric cryptography needs replacement.

Harvest now, decrypt later

Recording is free and undetectable

An adversary who captures ciphertext from a cable, a satellite beam or a radio link stores it until a quantum computer exists. Nothing in your logs will ever show it happened.

Mosca's inequality

X + Y > Z means you are late

Secrecy lifetime plus migration time versus time-to-quantum. Clinical trials, health records, land registries, defence imagery and 20-year spacecraft missions are already past the threshold.

Where it bites hardest

Broadcast, long-lived, hard to rekey

Space, aeronautical and subsea links are interceptable without breaching anything, run for decades on unchangeable hardware, and cannot be visited by an engineer.

The permanent core. Grover reduces a 256-bit search to 2128 operations — and Landauer's principle puts that beyond any physically realisable energy budget. A 256-bit symmetric key is a permanent solution, not a transitional one. Every constrained profile in this framework resolves to symmetric cryptography for exactly that reason.
Architecture

Four layers

v0.1.2 had seven pillars on a single plane. v0.2.0 adds a quantum-safe cryptographic core beneath them and a transport-domain layer above them, because the same pillar means something different on a subsea cable and on an acoustic modem.

The central idea

The profile is chosen by the physics of the link

Not by the sensitivity of the data. A framework that ignores this is quietly disregarded by the engineers who operate constrained links — so v0.2.0 defines three profiles and assigns every domain to one.

Profile Q-A

Unconstrained

Full hybrid post-quantum handshake per session. Bandwidth is not a consideration.

  • X25519 + ML-KEM-768 · ML-DSA-65 · AES-256-GCM
  • Subsea cable systems, cloud, ground segment
  • Enterprise Wi-Fi, broadband SATCOM
Profile Q-B

Amortised

The asymmetric handshake happens rarely and off-link; many short sessions are ratcheted from the result.

  • Ground/broadband handshake → symmetric KDF ratchet
  • ACARS / VDL2 / HFDL aeronautical datalinks
  • Routine spacecraft telecommand and telemetry
Profile Q-C

Symmetric only

No asymmetric operation crosses the link at all. Quantum-safe by construction; the burden moves to key custody.

  • Physically injected 256-bit roots · KDF ratchet
  • Ascon-AEAD128 or AES-256-GCM
  • Underwater acoustic, deep space, legacy embedded

New in v0.2.0

Seven transport domains

Space

Broadcast links, decades of life, no engineer visits

CCSDS SDLS at the data link, BPSec end-to-end across relays, and a hash-based trust anchor that must stay credible for the whole mission.

  • A telecommand frame is 1 024 octets — sign batches, not commands
  • Pre-launch key injection under split knowledge and dual control
  • Satellite QKD is the only route to intercontinental QKD
  • The ground segment causes more real incidents than the space segment
Aeronautical

The only domain where a control can kill by working

Security must never remove availability from a safety-of-life function. Where a control cannot be made fail-safe, it belongs on a different aircraft domain.

  • One ML-DSA-65 signature ≈ 15 ACARS messages of shared airtime
  • Answer: ground handshake, symmetric ratchet in the air
  • ACARS is still largely plaintext — a today problem, not a quantum one
  • No path from passenger devices into the Aircraft Control Domain
Underwater — cables

~99 % of intercontinental traffic

Two independent encryption layers with independent key custody: Layer-1 optical under carrier custody, application-layer PQC under the data owner's.

  • Repeaters destroy quantum states — QKD cannot cross the Atlantic by cable
  • Landing stations and the management plane are the real tap points
  • Route and landing diversity is a security control, not an SLA
Underwater — wireless

Radio does not propagate in seawater

Sound travels at 1 500 m/s: 0.67 s of latency per kilometre, 80 bit/s to a few kbit/s. A single PQ handshake would occupy the channel for minutes.

  • Profile Q-C: injected keys, KDF ratchet, Ascon-AEAD128
  • Loss-tolerant replay windows; nonces that never repeat
  • Rekey by courier or UUV; zeroise on tamper
  • Surfacing vehicles are a DTN — same profile as deep space
Wireless

Transmission is publication

A €100 software-defined radio captures the ciphertext. The migration priority is ordered by recoverable value per captured byte.

  • Enterprise EAP-TLS first — it carries the whole network
  • 5G SUCI concealment uses ECIES: retroactively breakable
  • eSIM provisioning yields durable credentials
  • Wireless identifiers and Wi-Fi sensing are personal data
Storage

Data in transit that has stopped

The risk driver is retention, not sensitivity. AES-256 on disk is fine; the problem is that the key was transported under RSA.

  • Re-encrypt long-retention archives under PQ-wrapped keys
  • Per-record DEKs make crypto-erasure — and Article 17 — possible
  • Embeddings can be inverted: classify them as their source data
  • Any medium lasting >15 years outlives its cryptography

Migration

Deadlines that already exist

Post-quantum migration is not a cryptography project. It is an inventory and dependency project with a cryptographic conclusion — discovery alone is about 40 % of the effort.

NowCode and firmware signing quantum-safe (CNSA 2.0). PQC clauses in every procurement — hardware bought today outlives the deadline.
End 2026EU Member States begin transition (NIS Cooperation Group roadmap).
2028UK NCSC: discovery and cryptographic inventory complete.
2030NIST IR 8547: RSA-2048 and ECC-256 deprecated. EU: high-risk use cases migrated. The real deadline for long-lived data.
2035NIST IR 8547: classical asymmetric cryptography disallowed. EU and UK: migration complete.
Governance

Emerging technology, handled honestly

Distributed ledgers

A narrow tool, not a database

If mutually distrusting parties do not need an append-only shared history, a signed audit log with timestamping is cheaper, faster and lawful.

  • No personal data on any replicated ledger — commitments only
  • Erasure by destroying the salt, designed in from the start
  • PQ signatures from inception; ledgers publish keys forever
  • Blockchain voting is not endorsed, and the page says why
AI & autonomous agents

A fourth actor class

An AI system is a processor that cannot be interviewed, cannot be fully tested, and does not reliably follow instructions. Constrain it by architecture, not by prompt.

  • Every agent gets its own short-lived, attenuated identity
  • Capability scoping in the tool layer, never in a system prompt
  • Prompt injection is structural — separate trust domains
  • Permission-aware retrieval; embeddings are personal data
Research horizons

Where engineering ends

An explicit T1–T4 credibility scale separating physical law from unsolved engineering — and both from vendor fiction.

  • T1: latency, no-cloning, Landauer, Shannon — permanent walls
  • T2: quantum repeaters, practical FHE, device-independent QKD
  • T3: gravitational position attestation as an open hypothesis
  • T4: gravitational-wave comms and FTL signalling — rejected
Why the rejections matter. Naming what physics forbids protects organisations from procurement fraud — a more common and more expensive threat than any exotic attack described in this framework.
